\section{External Validity (Second Tool Library)}

We constructed a second, disjoint tool library (20 tools: finance / iot /
logistics, three equivalence groups; \texttt{tools\_ext/}) with 12 task
templates (\texttt{templates\_ext/}), re-ran the five template agents plus
ChainAware with library-agnostic inference
(\texttt{--use-generic-inference}), and evaluated the same four splits at a
reduced scale (Val N=100, Systematicity N=150, Productivity N=150,
Substitutability N=50 of the generated sets).

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
Agent & Val & Systematicity & Productivity & Substitutability \\
\midrule
ReAct / NativeFC / Reflexion / Plan-Execute & 1.00 & 0.43 & 0.00 & 1.00 \\
ChainAware & 1.00 & 0.63 & 0.57 & 1.00 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{TSR on the second tool library (generic inference). Every agent drops
from the i.i.d. validation split (1.00) to systematicity (0.43) and
productivity (0.00 for template agents; 0.57 for ChainAware), and ChainAware
is best on both compositional axes. Full per-agent values and the verdict are
in \texttt{results/external\_validity.json}.}
\label{tab:ext_validity}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Verdict and limitations.} This is \emph{directional} external
validity support: the qualitative claim that compositional splits are harder
than i.i.d. holds on a second tool library, and ChainAware remains best on
the compositional axes. Three limitations prevent a quantitative
CGG-ordering comparison: (i) the external validation split is saturated
(1.00 for all agents), so gap magnitudes are compressed; (ii) substitutability
is also saturated (1.00 for all agents) because generic inference matches
variant names in the natural-language prompt and the equivalence-aware
evaluator credits equivalent calls---the same mechanism that separates
ChainAware on the main benchmark is here available to every agent; and
(iii) the template-agent degeneracy (Reflexion$\equiv$ReAct,
NativeFC$\equiv$PlanExecute) carries over to the external runs. The main
benchmark's validation TSR (0.268) is unaffected by the external-validity
driver bug that suppressed validation scores in an earlier external run:
main results are produced by the original pipeline into
\texttt{results/*\_summary.json}, while external runs write only to
